# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

static HTML/CSS — user-chosen. Three self-contained single-file prototypes sharing `assets/images/`. No build step, no framework, no backend. A later migration of the winning prototype to Next.js is possible but not committed.

## Users

**Primary: institutional program buyers.** Recreation department directors, school district administrators, foundation program officers, and community-organization leaders in Connecticut (Greater Hartford) and South Florida. Their job: source a vendor who can staff and run youth enrichment programming — after-school, camps, tutoring, events — and defend that choice to a board, a mayor's office, or a superintendent. They arrive checking whether this vendor is real, credentialed, and has served peers like them.

**Secondary: parents and guardians** of PK–8th grade students seeking tutoring, after-school programs, learning pods, or summer camp for their child. They get a clear secondary path, not the headline.

## Product Purpose

Creativista Charm LLC provides creative youth programming and educational consulting: it designs, staffs, and runs enrichment for children where an organization or family cannot run it themselves. Success for this site is an inbound inquiry from a municipality, school, or foundation — a contract conversation started — with family inquiries as the secondary conversion.

## Positioning

A single operator serving both institutional and family demand across two disconnected regional markets (Greater Hartford, CT and South Florida), with a municipal and school-district client history a newer competitor cannot claim. "Creativista" is the company's own coinage — a person who gives birth to creativity; an innovator, designer, promoter, or follower of the latest creative ideas — built on the Spanish agentive suffix "-ista," the same construction as "fashionista," and used with deliberate humor.

## Operating Context

Buying happens by relationship and referral inside a small regional network of parks-and-recreation departments, school districts, and civic foundations. Evaluation is reputational: a prospect checks who else has hired this vendor before making contact. Contact is direct — email and phone to the owner — not through a portal, RFP system, or booking flow. Two separate phone lines serve the two regions. Recruiting instructors and tutors runs continuously and competes for space on the same site.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Services offered:** after-school programs; student learning pods (operated under the separate brand CreativistaPods.com); summer camps and enrichment; tutoring for grades PK–8th; youth and educational consulting.

**Constraints:**
- No ecommerce, booking, enrollment, scheduling, or payment functionality. Not in scope.
- No backend. Contact is `mailto:` and `tel:` links only.
- Two service regions with distinct phone numbers; a single mailing address (PO Box, West Hartford, CT).
- Learning Pods live on a separate domain and must link out, not be absorbed.
- Open roles change over time; the careers content must be editable without touching layout.

**Undecided:** whether the winning prototype migrates to a framework; whether the Media Kit becomes a hosted page or stays a downloadable asset.

## Product Principles

1. **Proof outranks promise.** The 19 named clients and eight press outlets are the strongest asset this business owns and the reason an institutional buyer keeps reading. They belong high on the page, not in a footer.
2. **Two audiences, one site, no confusion.** An institutional buyer and a parent want different things; each must find their path within one viewport of arriving, without the site apologizing for serving both.
3. **Warmth is a credential here, not a liability.** This company sells time with children. A site that reads as sterile B2B undercuts the product even for a municipal buyer.
4. **Two regions are a fact, not a footnote.** Connecticut and South Florida are separate markets with separate phone numbers; the design must make regional relevance immediate rather than making a Florida visitor guess.
5. **Say only what is true.** With no statistics on hand, credibility comes from specificity — real names, real places, real roles — never from manufactured numbers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No user-specific requirement was established beyond a general standard. Target WCAG 2.2 AA: the brand magenta and teal both fail contrast against white at body-text sizes and must be darkened for text use or restricted to large display type, fills, and accents.
